FPL 2026/27 · Opening Squad Guide

Building for the first
six gameweeks, not one

The model does not optimise for a single gameweek. The opening squad is selected to maximise expected points across GW1–6, which means the captain for GW1 is less important than which teams you have covered for a full fixture run. This guide shows the model's current direction — the players and teams it is weighting most heavily — and will be updated as the transfer window closes, injuries are confirmed, and prices are set.

How the projections here were made. These numbers come from the Dixon-Coles model trained on historical player and team data, using cold-start priors for new signings and newly promoted sides. They are not trained on this season's results yet — that happens after GW1. Treat them as directional, not precise. The model's first confident projections arrive after the first two or three gameweeks of real 2026/27 data.

Man City are the standout early-season pick. Bournemouth, Crystal Palace, and Coventry in the first three gameweeks is the kind of fixture run that makes premiums justified. GW4 at Manchester United is the first real test, and GW6 at Liverpool is the only genuinely difficult fixture in the block. The model weights this run heavily: Haaland and two City defensive or midfield assets is the default opening structure.

Arsenal play Coventry at home in GW1, which flatters their opening record, then face a tricky GW2 at Villa and GW3 home to Chelsea. The run improves significantly in GW4–6. Arsenal defenders and Gyökeres are high-confidence picks over the six-week block, even if GW2–3 are not as easy as the raw fixtures suggest.

Liverpool have a manageable opening until GW6 when City visit Anfield. Newcastle's horror start — Liverpool at home then Spurs away — makes their GW3–6 run of Bournemouth, Leeds, Hull, Coventry look almost too easy in context. Their assets are worth holding past the rough opening.

Budget picks: £4–5.5m

Budget picks free up the funds for premiums. The model targets budget players who either have genuine clean sheet probability (enabling premium attackers elsewhere) or have attacking returns from good fixtures at low cost. Avoid budget enablers who will ride the bench — always ask whether the saving is worth the trade-off in projected points.

Captain rotation: GW1–4

The model does not armband-chase — the same player who was captain last week is not automatically the correct choice this week. Below is the rotation the model's optimiser weights most heavily, assuming the default opening squad.

GW Pick Fixture and reason
1 Haaland (MCI) Home to Bournemouth. Highest projected individual return of any GW1 fixture. Standard captain.
2 Haaland (MCI) Away at Crystal Palace. Palace are difficult away but City's output still leads all projections.
3 Haaland (MCI) Home to Coventry City. The most straightforward captain pick of the opening block — consider alternative only if he has a knock.
4 Saka / Gyökeres Haaland travels to Man United; the model switches to Arsenal assets. Arsenal are away at Sunderland — not glamorous but the underlying expected goals gap is significant.

Chip timing in FPL 2026/27 is most dependent on the blank and double gameweek calendar, which will not be announced until mid-season. These are the model's prior expectations for when to hold or play each chip.

Save it for the GW with the most blanks — typically the round before a double gameweek. Converts a disaster into a viable score.

Watch list: subject to change

These are the players or situations the model is monitoring before finalising the opening squad. They will be updated in this article as pre-season news comes in.

  • Gyökeres (ARS) Cold-start projection significantly underrates him given his 40+ goal season at Sporting. Needs Arsenal GW1 starting confirmation — if he's in the XI, he's in the squad.
  • Donnarumma (MCI) If confirmed as City's #1 GK, his GW1–3 fixture run makes him arguably the best GK in the game for the opening block. Watch pre-season for lineup signals.
  • Semenyo (MCI) Moved from Bournemouth to City. His role in City's system — starter or rotation — determines whether the £8.5m price tag is worth paying. Pre-season minutes are key.
  • Mount (MUN) Budget option that makes the squad work financially. His injury record means this is a "monitor until GW0" situation — the price is right, the risk is the same it's always been.
  • Calvert-Lewin (LEE) Projected 5.4 pts in GW1 at Nottingham Forest. Leeds' budget FWD slot may be worth it if he's fit and leads the line. Historically unavailable when you need him, but the price suggests they priced in the risk.
  • Newcastle assets Hall and any Newcastle midfielder are worth monitoring once GW1 vs Liverpool is out of the way. Their GW3–6 run (Bournemouth, Leeds, Hull, Coventry) is one of the cleanest in the league.
  • Transfer window (closes late Aug) Final week of the transfer window will confirm late arrivals and outgoings that change the value of many picks above. The model will re-project immediately after the window closes.
