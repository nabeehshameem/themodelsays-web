The World Cup 2026 record, match by match

All 104 matches, 2026-06-11 to 2026-07-19. Graded from the committed record — every figure on this page is computed from per_match.csv, not typed in.

We predicted every match of the 2026 World Cup before it was played, published each prediction in a public repository, and graded all 104 of them afterwards. This page is the whole record: the hits, the misses, and the two findings that made the model worse rather than better.

Finding one: retraining during the tournament made the model worse

We retrained after every matchday, weighting the tournament's own results four times more heavily than historical data. The intuition was obvious — use the newest information. Graded against the record, it was wrong.

ModelOutcome accuracy BrierExact scores
Frozen before the tournament 66.3% 0.5220 9.6%
Retrained after every matchday 59.6% 0.5406 11.5%

The version that never saw a single tournament result was 6.7 percentage points better across all 104 matches, and 15.6 points better in the knockouts — 68.8% against 53.1%. Three to seven matches per team is not enough data to update team strength; we were fitting to noise and calling it form. Walk-forward retraining has been dropped.

Finding two: the model could not imagine a well-organised underdog

Our confident predictions were not as good as their confidence implied. Predictions in the 90–100% band (6 of them) came in at 50.0%. A calibrated model's 90% predictions come in about 90% of the time.

Nearly all of the damage sat in one place: heavy favourites held to draws by sides that set up to defend. A Poisson goal model with a draw correction still assumes both teams are trying to score at their usual rate. That is the wrong assumption for a tournament with a 48-team field and a lot of underdogs happy to take a point.

Verify any of this yourself

Predictions were committed to a public repository before kick-off, so the timestamps are checkable independently of anything we say. The grading script reconstructs each prediction from the model parameters as they existed at least sixteen hours before the match — a walk-forward rebuild from git history, not a retrospective fit:

git clone https://github.com/nabeehshameem/fpl-optimiser
cd fpl-optimiser
python wc/retrospective/grade_retrospective.py

That regenerates per_match.csv and summary.txt, which are the only inputs to this page. If your numbers differ from the ones above, the numbers above are wrong.

What this record does and does not show. It shows that these predictions existed before the matches and how they scored. It does not show that the model will do as well on the next tournament — 104 matches is a small sample, and two of the model's weaknesses only became visible after the fact. The next public record is the 2026/27 Fantasy Premier League season, where every squad is committed before the deadline and graded afterwards on the same terms.

Every match

Two different predictions are graded here, and they can disagree. Top scoreline is the single most likely score. W/D/L call is the most likely outcome, which is every home-win scoreline added together against every draw and every away win. The most likely single score is often a draw while the most likely outcome is still a home win, because home victories are spread across many different scorelines.

That is why 5 matches below show a correct exact score next to a missed W/D/L call. Canada 1–1 Bosnia & Herzegovina is the clearest case: the top scoreline was 1–1, but the home win held 42% against the draw's 32%, so the outcome call was a home win. Both grades are shown rather than the flattering one.

Probabilities are home/draw/away at 90 minutes. Matches marked * went to extra time and are graded on the 90-minute result. Brier score is per match, lower being better.
